About The Ralf

A home away from home

The Ralf was created around a simple belief:
Hospitality should feel personal.
Design should feel intentional.
And every guest should feel known.

Whether it’s your first stay or your fifth, our mission is simple: make you feel known and welcomed, every time.

Our Philosophy

A guesthouse, not another hotel

We never set out to build another hotel. We set out to create a guesthouse. A place where thoughtful design, warm hospitality and the character of Old Montréal come together under one roof.

Somewhere between the comfort of home and the polish of a luxury stay.

Why a Guesthouse?

Only ten suites

With only ten suites, every stay feels personal. Fewer rooms mean more space. More attention. More familiarity.

It’s hospitality at a human scale.

The Wolf

More than our symbol

Throughout the house you’ll notice a wolf. It’s more than our symbol. It represents the values that guide everything we do: quiet confidence, loyalty, curiosity, and a strong sense of home.

Like the best hosts, it doesn’t ask for attention. It simply makes you feel welcome.

Our Goal

What we're building

  • 01Maintain an intimate, guesthouse feel with only 10 rooms, including one 2,000 sq. ft. penthouse.
  • 02Deliver design-led spaces that are inspiring, carefully curated, and quietly surprising.
  • 03Honour our centrally located heritage building, putting guests steps from the best of Old Montréal.
  • 04Build a reputation for warm, personal, discreet service that keeps guests coming back.

Our Values

What guides the house

Personal

We believe every guest should feel expected, known and welcomed.

Thoughtful

Every detail has a purpose. From the architecture to the bedding.

Curious

The best experiences aren’t always on a map. That’s why we created Just Ask Ralf.

Discreet

Luxury doesn’t need to announce itself. It should simply feel effortless.

Rooted

Old Montréal isn’t our backdrop. It’s our inspiration.

Some guests ask us who Ralf really was.

We like to think every stay offers a different answer.

After all, the best stories are never completely told.
